Dear Editors,

Please consider my Research Letter entitled
``Reader Disagreement Identifies Residual Diagnostic Risk in Automated
Arrhythmia ECG Interpretation'' for publication in
\textit{JACC: Clinical Electrophysiology}.

This retrospective secondary analysis examined whether disagreement between cardiologists 
identifies ECGs with persistent diagnostic difficulty despite adjudication. 
Using the publicly available CODE datasets, ECGs with reader disagreement 
showed substantially greater patient-level diagnostic error risk than consensus 
ECGs, and this excess risk persisted across retained coverage despite 
uncertainty-based referral reducing overall error burden. These findings 
suggest that reader disagreement carries clinically relevant information about 
interpretive difficulty that is lost when expert assessments are collapsed into 
a single adjudicated reference label.

The study used only publicly available, de-identified data. No additional
ethical or institutional review board approval was obtained for this secondary
analysis. The source CODE study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee
of the Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais
(protocol 49368496317.7.0000.5149).
